Chapter One

The Promise

Clinical trials are how medicine moves forward. They test whether a new drug, device, or treatment actually works — and whether it's safe — before it reaches the people who need it most. Every approved therapy on the market today passed through this gauntlet.

But a trial is only as good as the people inside it. If the participants don't reflect the patients who will eventually take the drug, the results can't tell us how it will really perform for everyone.

So who actually makes it into the room? This is the story of one fictional trial, and the 9,688 people who didn't.

Chapter Three

Where Are the Sites?

Trial sites cluster in metro areas. Patients don't. The average person lives 49 miles from the nearest site — and for rural patients, that's often a two-hour round trip for a 20-minute visit.
